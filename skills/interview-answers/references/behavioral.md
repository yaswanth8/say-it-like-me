# Behavioral, intro and HR answers

## "Tell me about a time..."

Spoken shape, about 60 seconds, five to seven sentences:

1. **Situation** in one or two sentences: where, what was at stake. Name the client or domain only if the profile does.
2. **What I did**, the longest part: decisions and actions, with "I". Use "we" only for things the team did together.
3. **Result**: what changed. Words, or a real number from the user. Never an invented one.
4. Optionally one sentence on what I'd keep doing. Not a slogan.

No "Situation / Task / Action / Result" labels in the answer; the structure should be felt, not announced.

### Where the stories come from

1. The profile's `## Interview stories` section, if it exists. Pick the story whose "Use for" matches the question; one story can answer several questions from different angles.
2. Otherwise, the resume facts in the profile (projects, upgrades, migrations, incidents, tools introduced). Build the closest true story and, after the answer, add one line: "Swap in the real details: <what to check>." Offer the story setup once.

### Common questions and what they're really asking

| Question | What they want to hear |
|---|---|
| Tell me about yourself | 60-90 seconds: current role and what I own, two highlights that match this job, why I'm looking at this role. Not the whole resume in order. |
| Hardest technical problem | How I debug and reason, not how hard it was. |
| Production incident | Calm triage, finding the root cause, the fix that stops it recurring. |
| Disagreement / conflict | Disagreeing with data and respect, and committing once a decision is made. |
| Tight deadline | Cutting scope deliberately and communicating early. |
| Mistake / failure | Owning it plainly, the fix, what changed after. Pick a real but survivable mistake. |
| Weakness | A real one with what I'm doing about it. Not "I'm a perfectionist". |
| Mentoring / leadership | Knowledge transfer, reviews, unblocking others. |
| Why are you leaving / why this company | Forward-looking: what I want to do more of. Never negative about the current employer. |

## Intro ("Walk me through your background")

> Current title and years, the stack in one breath, the current project and what I own on it, one earlier role in one sentence, and why this role fits.

Use the profile's roles and stated years. Keep the employment gap explanation (for example a master's degree) to one neutral sentence, and only if the profile has one.

## HR and screening

Answer from the profile's standard answers in one or two sentences: work authorization, sponsorship, notice period, start date, relocation, remote / hybrid preference, background check. When the profile says TODO (salary expectation, for example), don't make up a figure: tell the user it isn't set and suggest a way to answer without a number ("I'm looking for something in line with the market for this role; what range did you have budgeted?"), and ask them for their real number once.
